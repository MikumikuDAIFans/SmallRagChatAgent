This guide helps you understand and use toolpaths and G-code in the current project to achieve more stable and controllable machining results. Follow the steps—no technical background required.

## What Are Toolpaths?

- Toolpaths are the device’s motion routes on the material, turning your design into executable machining paths.
- Different machining modes (engraving, scribing, cutting, relief) use different movement patterns and sequences.
- Simply put: a toolpath is the machine’s plan for where to go next.

## Which Tool Should I Use?

- Choose by material: for example wood, acrylic, leather, etc.
- Choose by desired effect: small designs with many details or dense lines suit finer tools; large areas suit more robust tools.
- Prioritize stability: worn or poor-condition tools affect results; replace or service as needed.
- Test small samples first: performance varies by material–tool combinations; start with a small trial.

## Toolpath Generation Fails or Shows Errors?

- Check design clarity: overly complex images/graphics or unclear edges can hinder generation.
- Simplify content: remove unnecessary fine elements and complex backgrounds; keep key shapes and outlines.
- Confirm boundaries: ensure your design is within the machine’s workable area.
- Save and retry: save the project, then rerun the generation process.
- Close and reopen: close the current project or page, re-enter, and try again.

## Toolpath Simulation Looks Incorrect?

- Re-preview: switch preview modes or zoom in/out; focus on edges and transition regions.
- Clear occlusions: hide or remove unneeded layers and elements to avoid interference.
- Simplify design: overly complex patterns may be unclear in simulation; streamline and try again.
- Validate with a small cut: run a short real test to confirm alignment with expectations.

## G-code Fails to Load or Shows Errors?

- Confirm file integrity: avoid files from unknown sources or files that have been altered.
- Avoid special characters: use common characters in filenames and paths; avoid overly long or complex names.
- Re-export: export again from the current software to ensure correct format and content.
- Process stepwise: for very large files, import and execute in parts or stages.
- Reload: close the page or project, then load the file again.

## Can I Use G-code from Other Software?

- For compatibility and stability, prefer files generated by this software.
- If using other software’s files, ensure they are suitable for your device and from trusted sources.
- Test small samples first: verify safety and results on a small area before full runs.
- If incompatibility or anomalies occur, regenerate with this software.

## How to Save Processing Time?

- Simplify patterns: reduce unnecessary fine textures and dense linework.
- Arrange intelligently: group similar areas to reduce back-and-forth movement.
- Preview first: identify unnecessary motions or redundant paths and optimize in advance.
- Batch processing: lay out and run identical designs together to improve efficiency.
- Confirm with small tests: start with a small area to avoid rework and repeated attempts.
