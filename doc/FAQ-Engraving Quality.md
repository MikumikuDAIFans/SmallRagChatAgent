This guide helps you identify and improve engraving quality in the current project. Follow the steps—no technical background required.

## Uneven Engraving Depth?

- Choose images or patterns with clear tonal hierarchy; avoid overlapping very dark or very bright areas.
- Preview light–dark transitions in software to ensure critical details are visible.
- Keep materials flat and firmly secured to avoid warping or wobble affecting depth.
- Simplify fine textures; reduce large areas of dense lines to improve stability.
- Run a small-area test first; confirm consistent depth before full processing.

## Rough Edges or Burrs?

- Use graphics with sharp edges and higher contrast to reduce blur.
- Zoom into key contours in the preview; modestly increase contrast for crisper edges if needed.
- Avoid overly dense thin lines and overly complex patterns; keep linework simple.
- Remove unnecessary layers or occlusions to reduce interference that causes edge issues.
- Validate edge quality with small samples; adjust the artwork or composition if results are poor.

## Tool Breakage During Operation — Prevention

- Use tools in good condition; replace worn or abnormal tools promptly.
- Ensure materials are firmly fixed and the work surface is tidy to avoid collisions or sudden loads.
- Avoid processing very large areas in one pass; complete in stages for stability.
- For complex or harder materials, run small trials first to confirm safety and results.
- Keep the surrounding environment orderly to prevent accidental contact or obstruction.

## Engraving Too Slow — Optimization

- Simplify the pattern; reduce unnecessary fine textures and dense lines.
- Group similar areas together to reduce back-and-forth machine movement.
- Use preview to identify and remove unnecessary motions or redundant toolpaths.
- Batch layout and execution for identical designs to improve overall efficiency.
- Process key areas first, confirm results, then expand to full coverage to reduce rework.

## Improving Engraving Accuracy

- Choose clear source images and emphasize the subject; avoid complex backgrounds that dilute detail.
- Zoom in during preview to inspect key details (edges, textures) and ensure they are visible.
- Modestly increase overall contrast and tonal separation so outlines are easier to detect.
- Keep materials flat and firmly secured to reduce movement and error sources.
- Sample verification: confirm appearance and dimensions on a small area before full-scale processing.
